In a bustling café in downtown Tokyo, two old friends met after years apart. Sarah, an American journalist, and Yuki, a Japanese software engineer, had been college roommates in Boston.

"Yuki! It's been so long!" Sarah exclaimed, hugging her friend tightly.

"Ten years," Yuki replied, her eyes glistening. "You haven't changed at all."

They sat down and ordered coffee. Sarah looked around the café, admiring the minimalist Japanese design.

"So, tell me everything," Sarah said. "Last I heard, you were working for a big tech company."

Yuki nodded. "I was, for five years. Then I quit to start my own company. We develop AI-powered translation tools."

"That's amazing! What made you take that leap?"

"Remember when we traveled to Europe after graduation? We couldn't communicate with anyone. I thought, there has to be a better way. So I decided to build it."

Sarah laughed. "I remember! We got lost in Paris because we couldn't read the signs. What about you? Still writing?"

"Yes, but I've shifted focus. I used to cover politics, now I write about technology and society. It's fascinating how technology is changing our lives."

They talked for hours, sharing stories of their careers, families, and dreams. Yuki showed Sarah photos of her two children, while Sarah shared stories from her reporting trips around the world.

"You know," Sarah said, "I've been thinking about writing a book. About how technology connects people across cultures."

"That's a wonderful idea," Yuki said. "And I have plenty of stories for you. Every day at work, I see how our tools help people communicate. It's not just about words; it's about understanding."

As they left the café, the sun was setting over Tokyo. The city lights began to twinkle, and the streets filled with people heading home.

"Let's not wait another ten years," Sarah said.

"Agreed," Yuki replied. "Next time, you'll come to my house. My kids want to meet the famous journalist."

Sarah smiled. "And you'll come to New York. I'll show you the city that never sleeps."

They hugged goodbye, promising to stay in touch. As Sarah walked back to her hotel, she thought about how technology, ironically, had brought them back together after years of separation.
