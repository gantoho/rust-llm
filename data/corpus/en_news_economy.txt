BEIJING, Jan. 15 (Xinhua) — China's economy grew 5.2 percent year-on-year in 2024, exceeding the annual target of around 5 percent, official data showed on Wednesday.

The country's gross domestic product (GDP) reached 126 trillion yuan (about 17.8 trillion U.S. dollars) in 2024, according to the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS).

The growth rate was higher than the global average and demonstrated the resilience and vitality of the Chinese economy amid complex domestic and international conditions.

In the fourth quarter of 2024, the economy expanded by 5.2 percent year-on-year, accelerating from the 4.9 percent growth in the third quarter.

The service sector remained a key driver of growth, with its added value increasing by 5.8 percent for the whole year. The contribution of the service sector to GDP growth exceeded 60 percent.

Consumer spending continued to recover, with total retail sales of consumer goods reaching 47.1 trillion yuan, up 7.2 percent year-on-year. Online retail sales grew 11 percent to 15.4 trillion yuan, while sales of new energy vehicles and smart home appliances showed strong momentum.

Fixed-asset investment grew 3 percent to 50.3 trillion yuan. High-tech industry investment surged 10.3 percent, significantly outpacing overall investment growth. Manufacturing investment rose 6.5 percent, indicating growing confidence in the real economy.

Foreign trade remained stable, with the total value of goods imports and exports reaching 41.8 trillion yuan. Exports grew 0.6 percent while imports declined 0.3 percent, resulting in a trade surplus of 5.9 trillion yuan, a record high.

Resident income continued to grow steadily. Per capita disposable income reached 39,218 yuan, a real increase of 6.1 percent. Rural residents' income grew faster than that of urban residents, narrowing the urban-rural income gap.

The employment situation remained stable, with 12.44 million new urban jobs created throughout the year, exceeding the annual target. The average surveyed urban unemployment rate was 5.2 percent, down 0.4 percentage points from the previous year.

Consumer prices remained mild, with the consumer price index (CPI) rising 0.2 percent year-on-year. The producer price index (PPI) for industrial products fell 3.0 percent, reflecting some pressure in the industrial sector.

Looking ahead to 2025, economists generally expect China's economy to maintain stable growth. The implementation of a series of policy measures to stabilize growth is expected to support consumption, investment, and exports.

The NBS stated that China will continue to pursue progress while maintaining stability, fully and faithfully apply the new development philosophy, accelerate the construction of a new development pattern, and promote high-quality development to make a good start in building a modern socialist country in all respects.

The strong economic performance has boosted confidence in China's development prospects. International organizations including the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank have recently revised upward their forecasts for China's economic growth in 2025.

China's economic achievements in 2024 were hard-won, given the challenging external environment and domestic structural adjustments. The country's ability to maintain relatively rapid growth while advancing structural reforms and risk prevention demonstrates the effectiveness of its macroeconomic policies.

As the world's second-largest economy, China's economic performance has significant implications for global growth. The country contributed approximately 30 percent to global economic growth in 2024, remaining the largest contributor to world economic development.
